# Mobile critique — apps/web

Method: dual-agent (A: MobileDesignAFull · B: MobileEvidenceBFull)
Live Chromium inspection at 390×844, 360×800, and 844×390: feed, source picker, trust warning, search, filters, watch, Library, navigation and sign-in dialogs. No application code changed.

## Design specificity verdict
Coherent, but largely category-interchangeable. Dark cards, purple accents, category pills and Home/Shorts/Library/More communicate “video app” immediately. Nostube’s distinctive capabilities—relay selection, trust filtering and zaps—are much less understandable: globe, shield and lightning icons carry most of the explanation.
The opportunity is not a visual redesign. It is making which feed you’re viewing, what is filtered, and what an action does legible on a small screen.
Detector evidence: the broad scan returned 167 findings, all in generated dist-instance/ files or public embed demo/test pages—not the mobile app source. Narrow source scans returned zero, but were regex-only and cannot establish accessibility. Live scans found genuine contrast failures and the 8px BETA label. Background-card heading warnings under open dialogs were discarded as noise.
Overlay limitation: overlays were captured in a headless tab after a tab-local CSP bypass. No overlay remains visible in your browser; screenshots are the record.

## Overall impression
The core browse-to-watch flow works, and layouts do not spill horizontally. The biggest weakness is interaction clarity, not styling: controls look actionable without delivering a clear next step, while important state is visually weak or hidden.

## What’s working
- Core touch targets: Search, Sign in, Back, Share and dialog Close measure 44×44; bottom navigation items are 64px tall. Filter radio rows have full-size clickable labels.
- Reversible source browsing: selected relays are shareable through ?relay=, with explicit “Back to My feed,” “Save on this device” and “Copy link.”
- Content-first watch layout: playback worked; landscape player measured 812×312. Watch hides bottom navigation, and the landscape sign-in dialog scrolls with Close reachable.

## Priority issues
### 1. [P1] Guest watch actions look usable but fail to guide the viewer
The thumb icons are static when signed out. Only the adjacent reaction counts open the reaction list; those buttons measured 8–9×44px, with accessible names consisting only of a number. Zap produces “Sign in to support creators with a zap” without a sign-in action.
Why it matters: tapping the apparent action does nothing, while an adjacent action reports an error. Screen-reader users cannot identify what the count button means.
Fix: separate reaction-list access from membership actions. Give count controls meaningful labels and adequate width; make guest like/zap interactions open the existing sign-in flow with context.
Source: apps/web/src/components/VideoReactionButtons.tsx:171–205; apps/web/src/hooks/useZap.ts:61–63.
Suggested command: /impeccable onboard

### 2. [P1] Important dark-theme text fails contrast
Measured contrast: active bottom-navigation label 2.6:1; Sign-up link 2.7:1; trust-warning confirmation text 2.56:1. These fall below the 4.5:1 threshold for normal-size text. The watch creator’s trust-details button is also only 12×12px.
Why it matters: current location, account entry and a consequential confirmation are harder to read or activate.
Fix: use a lighter foreground accent for dark-theme links and selected navigation, independently of filled-button branding. Correct destructive-button foreground/background contrast. Expand the trust badge’s hit area without enlarging its icon.
Source: packages/widgets/src/theme.css:47–55; apps/web/src/components/MobileBottomNav.tsx:45–52; apps/web/src/components/TrustBadge.tsx:62–76.
Suggested command: /impeccable harden

### 3. [P2] Feed controls consume space while hiding feed identity
At 360px, only roughly three categories are visible. A long selected-relay label squeezes that further. The relay-status card can push the first thumbnail to approximately y=530/800. The category/source row scrolls away despite its sticky declaration. On landscape Home, header, install banner, controls and bottom navigation leave only about 86px of thumbnails visible.
Why it matters: viewers spend scarce screen space on controls, then lose the source context once browsing.
Fix: give source and trust a compact, labeled row separate from categories. Reduce the relay-status card to a summary with disclosure. Keep source identity available without making all feed controls permanently sticky.
Source: apps/web/src/components/CategoryButtonBar.tsx:40,89–94; apps/web/src/pages/HomePage.tsx:137–140.
Suggested command: /impeccable layout

### 4. [P2] Search refinement hides its own feedback
Results show the query as a heading rather than an editable field. Filters apply live beneath an approximately 85%-height sheet, but the sheet provides no result feedback. After closing, “Filter 1” identifies quantity, not the selected constraint.
Why it matters: users must remember their choices and close the sheet to judge their effect.
Fix: retain an editable, prefilled query; show named removable filter chips; add a persistent “Show results” dismissal control with the currently loaded result count. Keep live filtering—an extra Apply transaction is unnecessary.
Source: apps/web/src/pages/SearchPage.tsx:141–178; apps/web/src/components/SearchFiltersButton.tsx:186–211.
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

### 5. [P2] Trust confirmation uses alarm without clear decision hierarchy
“You are entering the danger zone” and liability-style wording dominate the dialog. The filled red “I understand, disable it” appears above the outlined “Keep filter on.”
Why it matters: the warning creates anxiety while visually emphasizing the riskier choice.
Fix: retain the explicit content warning, but title it “Show unfiltered videos?” and explain the concrete change. Make “Keep filter on” visually primary; make disabling secondary and deliberate.
Source: apps/web/src/hooks/useTrustFilter.tsx:133–177.
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

## Cognitive load and emotional journey
Moderate load: 3 of 8 checks fail—competing first-screen chrome, numerous refinement choices, and remembering source/filter state. Grouping, card hierarchy and progressive disclosure are generally sound.
The journey starts well with rich thumbnails and straightforward playback. Friction peaks at the trust warning and guest-action dead ends.
A separate playback failure appeared once during A’s inspection; Retry restored playback. Its raw-URL error presentation needs improvement, but the underlying failure was not diagnosed. A successful HTTP HEAD request does not prove playable media or rule out network/CORS/decoding problems.
